Key idea: The cloud is not a literal cloud in the sky. It is a way of using computers and storage operated by an online service.

1. What is cloud storage?

Cloud storage means keeping data on computers operated by a service provider and accessing that data through a network, usually the internet.

The computers are usually located in data centres. A data centre is a real building containing computer systems, storage equipment, power systems, cooling, and security measures. Some data centres may be underground or specially protected, but not all of them are.

Important: “The cloud” does not mean one single server or one single building. Online providers may use many computers and facilities.

2. Three ideas that people often confuse

Cloud storage

Data is kept on computers operated by an online service.

Example: You upload a document and later open it through the service’s website or app.

Synchronisation

Selected information is updated across connected devices or services.

Example: You add a calendar appointment on your phone and it appears on your laptop.

Backup

A separate copy is kept so that information may be recovered if something goes wrong.

Example: You restore photographs after losing a phone.

Remember: Synchronisation is designed to keep selected information up to date. Backup is designed to help you recover information. A synchronised copy is not automatically a complete or independent backup.

Why can synchronisation sometimes be risky?

If you delete a file or make an unwanted change, that change may also appear on other synchronised devices. A separate backup may help, depending on how the service stores deleted or older versions.

5. Benefits

Access

You may be able to access supported files from different devices and locations.

Recovery

A cloud copy may help after a device is lost, damaged, or replaced.

Sharing

Online services can make it easier to share selected files with other people.

Flexible working

People may work from home, an office, or another location.

Access depends on the service, the account, permissions, the files that were uploaded, and the availability of a network connection.

6. Risks and limitations

Internet or network failure

A network outage, damaged cable, router problem, or mobile-network failure can delay access and synchronisation.

Account takeover

If someone obtains your password or access to your account, they may be able to view, change, or delete data.

Privacy

Photos, documents, activity, and location information can be sensitive. Review who can access them.

Service outage

An online service can become temporarily unavailable. No online system is risk-free.

Accidental deletion

A deletion or unwanted change may synchronise to other devices. An independent backup can help.

Serious disruption

Fire, flood, power failure, cyberattack, terrorism, or war could affect networks or facilities. Providers use protection and redundancy, but interruptions remain possible.

Android and Google location services

Some Android and Google services may use location information when the relevant settings and permissions are enabled. Examples can include navigation, finding a device, location sharing, and some activity history features.

This does not mean that every Google service uses location in the same way or that every Android user is continuously tracked in the same manner. Review location, app, account, and sharing settings.

Good security habits: Use a strong, unique password, turn on two-step verification, be careful with unexpected links, update devices and apps, and check sharing permissions.

7. Alternatives to cloud storage

No single storage method is perfect. Important information should not exist in only one place.

Comparing ways to store data
Option Advantages Limitations
Cloud storage Access from different places, sharing, and possible recovery after device loss. Depends on an account, provider, permissions, and usually an internet connection. Privacy and cost must be considered.
Computer or phone storage Fast and usually works offline. The device can be lost, damaged, stolen, or fail.
External hard drive Large capacity and works without the internet. Can fail, be lost, stolen, damaged, or infected by malware. It should not be the only copy.
USB flash drive Small, portable, and works offline. Easy to lose or damage. It is not automatically a safe backup.

A sensible plan often uses more than one method. Keep an independent copy of important files and test that the copy can be opened or restored.

8. Build a sensible plan

  1. Decide which information is important.
  2. Check what your cloud service actually stores or synchronises.
  3. Use a strong, unique password.
  4. Turn on two-step verification.
  5. Review location, app, account, and sharing permissions.
  6. Keep an independent copy of important files.
  7. Disconnect an external backup drive when it is not being used.
  8. Test that backups can be opened or restored.
  9. Keep essential information available offline where practical.
  10. Keep your phone, computer, and apps updated.

Why does the app use semantic HTML?

Elements such as <main>, <nav>, <section>, <article>, and <footer> describe the purpose of each part of the page. This helps learners, browsers, search tools, and assistive technologies understand the structure.

Why is alternative text important?

Alternative text describes the purpose of an image for people who cannot see it or who are using a screen reader. Decorative images can have empty alternative text so they are not announced unnecessarily.

Glossary

Cloud storage
Keeping data on computers operated by an online service.
Synchronisation
Updating selected information across connected devices or services.
Backup
A separate copy intended to help recover information.
Data centre
A building containing computer systems and storage equipment.
Two-step verification
An additional security check used after entering a password.
Location service
A feature that can estimate a device’s location using information such as GPS, Wi-Fi, mobile networks, or sensors.

Final message

Cloud storage is useful online storage. Synchronisation keeps selected information updated. Backup gives you another way to recover data. A sensible plan uses the right combination for the information you need to protect.
